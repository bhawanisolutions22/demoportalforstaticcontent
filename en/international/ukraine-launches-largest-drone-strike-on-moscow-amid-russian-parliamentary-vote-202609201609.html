Ukraine Launches Largest Drone Strike on Moscow Amid Russian Parliamentary Vote

Ukraine carried out what Moscow officials described as the largest drone attack yet on the Russian capital, coinciding with the final day of Russia’s parliamentary elections.

According to Moscow Mayor Sergei Sobyanin, the strikes killed two people in the Moscow region and two more in Russian‑held Kherson, including an election official. A facility at Moscow’s oil refinery was also damaged. Sobyanin said the attack was “clearly planned to disrupt the elections” but claimed it was repelled.

The State Duma vote, the first since Russia’s full‑scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, is expected to reaffirm the dominance of the ruling United Russia party. The Kremlin is likely to present the outcome as evidence of public support for President Vladimir Putin’s war policies.

Despite cyberattacks and restrictions on opposition candidates, turnout exceeded 45% by Sunday morning. Liberal party Yabloko attempted to tap into war fatigue, but its leaders faced repression, including long jail terms for senior figures.

Meanwhile, regional tensions escalated as Saudi Arabia intercepted a missile aimed at Riyadh, and Yemen’s Houthi movement claimed attacks on Saudi targets. The US State Department renewed its travel alert for Americans in West Asia, warning of rapid escalation potential.

Initial election results were expected Sunday evening, with full outcomes due Monday.

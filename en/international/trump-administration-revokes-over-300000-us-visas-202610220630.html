Trump Administration Says It Has Revoked More Than 300,000 US Visas

Posted Date 22 Oct 2026

The administration of US President Donald Trump has revoked more than 300,000 visas since he returned to office in January 2025, the US State Department said on Tuesday. The announcement highlights the accelerating pace of the administration's immigration enforcement measures.

The latest figure represents a substantial increase from the department's previous update. On August 10, the State Department reported that more than 175,000 visas had been revoked since the beginning of Trump's second term.

The administration has defended its immigration policies as necessary to protect national security and improve public safety. However, civil rights organisations and immigration advocates have raised concerns about due process, freedom of expression and the treatment of minority communities.

State Department Defends Visa Revocations

Announcing the latest figures on social media platform X, State Department spokesperson Tommy Pigott said the administration had revoked visas at a faster rate than any previous US administration.

Pigott also emphasised the government's strict approach towards foreign nationals holding US visas who violate the law, commit fraud or are considered a threat to national security.

The administration has pursued a broad immigration crackdown, combining visa cancellations with deportation efforts and stricter screening procedures for people seeking to enter or remain in the United States.

Visa Decisions Draw Criticism From Rights Groups

Some of the administration's visa revocations have attracted criticism from civil liberties groups, particularly cases involving foreign nationals who participated in pro-Palestinian demonstrations against Israel's military campaign in Gaza.

Other controversial cases have involved foreign nationals whose social media comments about the assassination of conservative commentator Charlie Kirk drew the administration's attention.

Critics argue that these actions risk penalising individuals for expressing political opinions. They have also questioned whether national security concerns are being applied too broadly to people who criticise government policies.

The administration has additionally increased scrutiny of social media activity by immigrants and visa applicants as part of its wider immigration enforcement strategy.

Concerns Over Immigration Enforcement

Rights advocates have accused the government of using national security justifications to target critics and applying individual cases of alleged fraud to justify wider scrutiny of immigrant communities.

The Somali American community in Minnesota has been among the groups cited by critics raising concerns about the broader impact of these policies.

The administration, meanwhile, maintains that its approach is intended to enforce immigration laws and protect American interests.

Legal Immigration Also Faces Tighter Rules

Although Trump campaigned in 2024 on a promise to curb illegal immigration, his administration's policies have also made some legal immigration pathways more difficult.

Measures have included higher fees for certain work visas, alongside stricter screening and enforcement procedures.

The latest visa revocation figures underscore the scale of the administration's immigration campaign. The debate over these measures continues, with supporters emphasising law enforcement and national security while critics warn of potential consequences for civil liberties and legal immigration.

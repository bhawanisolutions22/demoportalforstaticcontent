Trump Plans New Sweeping Tariffs Despite Republican Midterm Election Setbacks: Report

US President Donald Trump is preparing to introduce another round of tariffs targeting major global trading partners, despite significant setbacks for his Republican Party in the recent US midterm elections, according to a Reuters report.

The proposed measures are expected to focus on what the Trump administration describes as excessive industrial production capacity across 16 major trading partners. The announcement could come shortly, with officials familiar with the matter indicating that the administration is moving ahead with its plans.

China is among the countries under scrutiny. The world's second-largest economy is expected to record a trade surplus exceeding $1 trillion in 2026. However, the proposed tariffs on Chinese goods are reportedly expected to remain within the limits established under the current trade agreement between Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping.

China Rejects US Overcapacity Allegations

Beijing has once again criticised Washington's approach, rejecting the suggestion that China's industrial production levels represent a problem for global trade.

Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun said that the concept of overcapacity was being used as a political argument and warned against using it to justify trade restrictions.

He also called on both countries to implement the agreements reached by their leaders and maintain stable economic and trade relations.

Despite China's objections, analysts expect the United States to maintain or potentially increase trade barriers against China and several other economies.

Political Setbacks May Not Change Trump's Trade Policy

The Republican Party's losses in the midterm elections have raised questions about whether Trump's trade strategy could face stronger political opposition.

However, analysts believe the election outcome may have limited influence on the administration's tariff policy.

The Democratic Party's gains in Congress could create additional scrutiny of Trump's economic decisions. Nevertheless, overturning presidential tariff measures could prove difficult because Congress would need sufficient support to overcome a presidential veto.

Economists have also pointed out that tariffs have contributed to higher prices for certain consumer goods in the United States, making trade policy an important issue for American households.

According to ING analysts, the election results are unlikely to signal a complete return to free trade, as tariffs in strategically important industries have received support from politicians across party lines.

US-China Trade Truce Remains Important

The existing trade understanding between Washington and Beijing is another major factor influencing the proposed measures.

The current US-China trade truce is scheduled to remain in place until January 10, although expectations are growing that both countries could extend the arrangement.

Trump and Xi are also expected to meet at a G20 gathering in Miami, potentially providing an opportunity to discuss trade relations and prevent another escalation.

Under the existing arrangement, Trump's second-term tariffs on Chinese products are capped at 20%, in addition to duties of approximately 25% introduced during his first presidential term.

The United States has also imposed a 12.5% anti-forced-labour duty on Chinese imports, leaving limited room before reaching the agreed second-term tariff ceiling.

Both countries are additionally working towards reducing tariffs on approximately $30 billion worth of non-strategic imports from each side.

Tariff Rates May Differ Across Countries

The proposed tariffs are not expected to be identical for every trading partner. According to people familiar with the discussions, Washington is considering country-specific rates based on existing trade arrangements and negotiations.

Mexico and Norway are reportedly unlikely to face immediate additional duties.

The administration is also expected to respect tariff ceilings included in recently finalised trade agreements with several partners.

  • European Union: 15% tariff ceiling.
  • Japan: 15% tariff ceiling.
  • South Korea: 15% tariff ceiling.
  • Malaysia: 19% tariff ceiling.
  • China: 20% cap on second-term tariffs, subject to continuation of the trade truce.

If the agreement with Beijing expires, China could face higher duties under the proposed excess-capacity measures.

India and Other Asian Economies Included in US Probe

The US investigation covers a broad group of trading partners across Asia, Europe and North America.

The economies included are China, India, Japan, South Korea, Mexico, Vietnam, Taiwan, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, Switzerland, Norway, Singapore, Bangladesh and Cambodia, along with the European Union.

The Office of the United States Trade Representative (USTR) launched its investigation into excess industrial capacity in March under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974.

A separate investigation is examining whether countries are adequately enforcing laws against forced labour.

Together, these investigations are intended to establish a legal basis for replacement tariffs after the US Supreme Court struck down Trump's earlier global tariff measures, commonly referred to as the 'Liberation Day' tariffs, in February.

China and Southeast Asian Manufacturing Under Scrutiny

The Trump administration has identified China as a major source of what it considers excess industrial capacity.

Washington argues that large-scale Chinese production is putting pressure on international markets through exports of steel, aluminium, electric vehicles, household appliances, solar equipment and other manufactured goods.

Beijing, however, maintains that its export performance reflects industrial competitiveness rather than harmful overproduction.

The US investigation is also examining Chinese investments in Southeast Asian countries, particularly Vietnam, Indonesia and Cambodia.

American officials are concerned that some Chinese companies may be shifting manufacturing operations to these countries to avoid existing US tariffs while continuing to supply the American market.

Global Trade Faces Renewed Uncertainty

The proposed tariff measures could introduce another period of uncertainty for international trade, particularly for countries with significant manufacturing and export links to the United States.

India and other Asian economies could face additional challenges if Washington moves ahead with country-specific duties.

The extent of the impact will depend on the final tariff rates, existing trade agreements and the outcome of negotiations between the United States and its trading partners.

For now, the possibility of fresh US tariffs highlights the continuing uncertainty surrounding global trade relations and the future direction of Washington's economic policy.

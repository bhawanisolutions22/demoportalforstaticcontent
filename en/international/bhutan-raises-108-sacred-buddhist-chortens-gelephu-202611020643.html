Bhutan Raises 108 Sacred Buddhist Chortens in Gelephu with 40,000 Volunteers

Bhutan has witnessed a remarkable religious and community gathering as approximately 40,000 volunteers came together to raise 108 sacred Buddhist chortens simultaneously in Gelephu Mindfulness City. The historic initiative brought together people from across the country in a shared expression of faith, devotion and voluntary service.

Each of the 108 structures stands 15 metres tall, making the undertaking a significant event in Bhutan's religious and cultural history.

108 Sacred Chortens Raised in a Single Day

The historic activity began at 3 AM local time following traditional Buddhist prayers and religious ceremonies. The commencement of the operation was marked by the powerful sound of 58 sets of traditional dungchen, long Buddhist horns used during important religious occasions.

The initiative, envisioned by His Majesty King Jigme Khesar Namgyel Wangchuck, involved volunteers working simultaneously at 108 designated locations to raise all the structures within a single day.

Thousands of people travelled to Gelephu to take part in the event, while many others gathered to witness the extraordinary occasion. The large-scale participation reflected Bhutan's longstanding tradition of Zhabto, a form of voluntary service for religious and community activities.

A 12-Kilometre Spiritual Corridor Along the Mau Chhu River

The 108 chortens have been strategically positioned along the banks of the Mau Chhu river, creating an approximately 12-kilometre spiritual corridor.

Each structure measures 15 metres in height, with a distance of 108 metres separating consecutive chortens. The arrangement gives the project both a distinctive physical scale and deep religious significance.

The number 108 holds special importance in Buddhist philosophy and is associated with spiritual purification, wisdom and the journey towards enlightenment.

Known as the Stupas of Enlightenment, the chortens are being prepared according to traditional Buddhist practices. Their sacred contents include religious relics, scriptures, prayers and other consecrated offerings.

Bhutanese Royal Family Leads Historic Religious Celebration

The main ceremony is taking place in the presence of the Bhutanese Royal Family, with His Holiness the Je Khenpo, Bhutan's highest spiritual authority, leading the religious observances.

His Majesty the King is also addressing the gathering, highlighting the spiritual and national significance of the initiative.

The occasion carries additional importance as it coincides with Lhabab Düchen, the Buddhist festival commemorating Lord Buddha's descent from the heavenly realm, as well as the 18th Coronation Anniversary of the King.

Four Days of Prayers and Religious Ceremonies

The celebrations are scheduled from 1 to 4 November and include several religious activities.

The programme features consecration prayers, sacred offerings and circumambulation of the newly raised chortens. These observances are intended to bless the structures and reinforce their spiritual importance.

The participation of thousands of people from different parts of Bhutan has also turned the occasion into a major national celebration of Buddhist faith and community unity.

India and Other Buddhist Communities Contribute to the Event

The initiative also highlights the shared Buddhist heritage connecting Bhutan with other countries across Asia.

Sacred relics and contributions have come from India and other Buddhist communities, reflecting the longstanding religious and cultural connections between these nations.

India is represented at the occasion by Union Minister for Parliamentary Affairs and Minority Affairs Kiren Rijiju. His participation highlights the close spiritual and cultural relationship between India and Bhutan, particularly their shared Buddhist traditions.

108 Chortens Symbolise Peace, Compassion and Unity

The raising of the 108 Jangchub Chortens represents more than a large-scale construction achievement. It reflects Bhutan's commitment to preserving its Buddhist heritage, strengthening community participation and promoting spiritual values.

The collective effort of approximately 40,000 volunteers has made the initiative a significant example of faith-driven voluntary service.

Once completed and consecrated, the 108 sacred chortens will stand as lasting symbols of peace, compassion, spiritual awakening and the well-being of all sentient beings.

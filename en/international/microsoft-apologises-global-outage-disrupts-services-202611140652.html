Microsoft Apologises After Global Outage Disrupts Services

Posted Date 14 Nov 2026

Microsoft has apologised for a major global service disruption that affected millions of customers and business partners, saying that its services were gradually returning to normal across impacted regions.

In a statement posted on X on Friday, the technology company expressed regret over the incident and acknowledged the responsibility it carries towards customers who depend on its platforms for their daily operations.

Microsoft said it understood the trust users place in its services and took that responsibility seriously. The company added that it was witnessing widespread recovery, with stability improving across its affected services.

The company clarified that the outage resulted from a technical problem and was not caused by a cyberattack or artificial intelligence (AI) agents.

According to Microsoft's initial assessment, the disruption originated during an earlier network capacity upgrade. A subsequent network configuration change triggered the underlying issue, affecting the availability of several services.

Microsoft said it had identified and addressed the technical problem and was continuing efforts to restore access for customers and regions still experiencing disruptions.

Services Restored Gradually

Microsoft explained that recovery was carried out in stages to ensure that services remained stable as systems came back online. The company conducted stability checks at each stage before restoring additional capacity.

Although this cautious approach extended the recovery period, Microsoft said it was necessary to prevent further disruptions and ensure reliable service availability.

The company also confirmed that it would publish a detailed post-incident report once its investigation and internal review were complete.

Earlier recovery updates had already indicated improvements in service availability and email delivery performance across a growing portion of the affected infrastructure.

Technical Issues Triggered Widespread Disruption

The outage began on Thursday night after server health-check failures caused several affected servers to become unavailable.

Microsoft initially attempted to resolve the problem by testing configuration changes and restarting the impacted servers. It subsequently introduced an alternative health-check mechanism, allowing some of the affected infrastructure to resume operations.

Despite these measures, the disruption continued into Friday, affecting businesses, airlines and government operations in several countries.

Users experienced difficulties accessing widely used Microsoft platforms, including Outlook, Microsoft Teams and its artificial intelligence assistant, Copilot.

The incident highlighted how heavily organisations across the world rely on Microsoft's cloud-based services and workplace platforms for communication, business operations and essential digital activities.

With services gradually recovering, Microsoft said it remained focused on restoring full stability and providing customers with a detailed explanation of the incident.

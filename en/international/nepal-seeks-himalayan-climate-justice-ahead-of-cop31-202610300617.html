Nepal Seeks Himalayan Climate Justice Ahead of COP31

Nepal is preparing to place Himalayan protection and climate justice at the centre of international climate discussions ahead of the upcoming United Nations Climate Change Conference, COP31. The country has identified climate-related loss and damage, accessible financial support and stronger international recognition of mountain ecosystems as key priorities for the negotiations.

Foreign Minister Shishir Khanal outlined Nepal's position during the National Climate Summit 2026 in Kathmandu on Thursday, highlighting the growing climate risks facing the Himalayan region and the need for a coordinated global response.

Khanal said climate change in the Himalayas should not be viewed as an issue affecting Nepal alone. According to him, the condition of the mountain ecosystem has far-reaching consequences for water security, agriculture, energy supplies and economic stability across the region and beyond.

Pointing to the increasing impact of floods and landslides, the Foreign Minister referred to the devastating disaster in the Bhote Koshi corridor in August. He said such climate-related events have caused extensive damage to communities, infrastructure, livelihoods and economic activity. Preliminary estimates put the reconstruction and rehabilitation requirement following the August disaster at approximately $4.7 billion.

Four Key Priorities for Nepal at COP31

Khanal identified four major areas that Nepal intends to highlight during the international climate negotiations.

These include securing greater recognition of Himalayan concerns in global climate discussions, addressing climate-induced loss and damage, ensuring fair and accessible climate finance, and making climate justice a central part of international climate action.

He also called for sustained global dialogue on mountain ecosystems and proposed a dedicated action plan for the Himalayas. Nepal wants the particular vulnerabilities of mountain regions to receive greater attention in international climate policy.

Khanal further emphasised the importance of achieving the Paris Agreement's target of limiting global warming to 1.5 degrees Celsius, warning that rising temperatures could have serious consequences for fragile mountain environments.

Nepal Seeks Greater Access to Climate Finance

Access to adequate climate funding is another major concern for Nepal as it prepares for COP31.

Citing the country's third Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC), Khanal said Nepal estimates its climate adaptation requirements at approximately $18 billion to $20 billion between 2025 and 2035.

He stressed that developing countries facing severe climate impacts need financial assistance that is not only sufficient but also accessible. Funding to address loss and damage caused by climate-related disasters must also reach affected countries and communities without unnecessary barriers.

Agriculture, Forest and Environment Minister Gita Chaudhary echoed these concerns, calling for climate justice to result in practical support rather than remain limited to international commitments and statements.

She urged developed countries and the wider international community to provide grants instead of loans to communities recovering from climate disasters. She also highlighted the importance of technology transfers and financial assistance to help vulnerable populations rebuild their lives.

Nepal and Bhutan to Raise Mountain Agenda Together

Chaudhary said Nepal and Bhutan are preparing to jointly promote mountain-related concerns at COP31. Their efforts will focus on securing greater international recognition of the Himalayas as a distinct priority in global climate negotiations.

She also stressed that climate adaptation programmes must reach people at the local level. Women, children, Indigenous Peoples and marginalised communities should have a meaningful role in climate policymaking and decisions affecting their future.

According to Chaudhary, climate policies must address the needs of vulnerable communities and ensure that the benefits of adaptation measures reach those most exposed to environmental risks.

COP31 to Take Place in Türkiye in November

The National Climate Summit 2026 is part of Nepal's preparations for COP31, scheduled to take place in Antalya, Türkiye, from November 9 to 20.

The Kathmandu summit has brought together relevant stakeholders to discuss climate challenges, consolidate the country's national position paper and establish policy priorities ahead of the international negotiations.

Nepal is seeking stronger global recognition of the challenges facing mountain ecosystems, particularly in countries that contribute relatively little to global greenhouse gas emissions but experience significant climate-related consequences.

Through its participation at COP31, Nepal aims to push for stronger Himalayan protection, improved adaptation support, accessible climate finance and meaningful action on climate-induced loss and damage.

The outcome of these discussions could help bring greater attention to the specific needs of mountain communities and strengthen the role of Himalayan countries in shaping future international climate policies.

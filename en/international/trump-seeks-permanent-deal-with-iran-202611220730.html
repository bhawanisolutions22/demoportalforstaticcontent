Trump Seeks Permanent Deal with Iran, Says Next Move Could Wait Until 2027

Posted Date 22 Nov 2026

US President Donald Trump has indicated that he is in no hurry to make his next move in the ongoing conflict with Iran, suggesting that a decision could potentially be delayed until 2027. At the same time, he has expressed his preference for reaching a lasting agreement with Tehran.

In an interview with an Israeli television network on Thursday, Trump said he was "not rushing" to decide on the next steps in the conflict. Speaking to Fox News on Saturday, he reiterated that he wanted a "permanent" deal with Iran rather than a temporary arrangement.

However, diplomatic efforts are unfolding alongside reports of possible further military action. According to sources familiar with the matter cited by CBS News, the United States and Israel are considering the possibility of launching another military assault on Iran in the near term.

The reported discussions come amid Israeli concerns over Iran's efforts to rebuild its weapons production capabilities. The administration of Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is reportedly monitoring how quickly Tehran could restore its military infrastructure.

Meanwhile, Iran has rejected allegations made by Trump that the country may be pursuing chemical or biological weapons. On Thursday, Iran's Foreign Ministry dismissed the accusations, arguing that US allegations were constantly changing and followed what it described as the "same fraudulent script" used to justify the 2003 US invasion of Iraq.

The latest developments highlight the uncertainty surrounding the conflict, with diplomatic negotiations, military considerations and competing accusations continuing to shape relations between Washington, Jerusalem and Tehran.

Whether Trump's preference for a permanent agreement can translate into meaningful negotiations remains uncertain, particularly as reports of possible renewed military action continue to emerge.

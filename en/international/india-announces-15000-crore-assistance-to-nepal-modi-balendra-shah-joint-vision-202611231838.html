India Announces ₹15,000 Crore Assistance to Nepal as Modi and Balendra Shah Set Joint Vision for Future Partnership

India has announced development assistance worth ₹15,000 crore to Nepal, marking a significant step towards strengthening economic and strategic ties between the two neighbouring countries. The package includes financial grants for reconstruction and infrastructure development, along with a rupee-denominated credit facility to support energy projects.

The announcement followed bilateral discussions between Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Nepalese Prime Minister Balendra Shah during the latter's two-day State Visit to India on November 22 and 23, 2026.

The two leaders also adopted a Joint Vision for a 'Viksit Bharat and Samunnat Nepal', setting out an ambitious roadmap for cooperation in economic development, transport connectivity, energy, water resources, climate resilience, disaster management, digital technology, space, innovation and cultural exchanges.

India to provide ₹3,500 crore in grants and ₹11,500 crore in credit

The development assistance package comprises ₹3,500 crore in grant assistance and ₹11,500 crore through a rupee-denominated Line of Credit.

The grant component will support reconstruction efforts following natural disasters and the development of new infrastructure projects. The credit facility will primarily finance projects in Nepal's energy sector.

Both governments will jointly identify the projects and finalise their implementation arrangements through consultations.

During the discussions, Prime Minister Balendra Shah acknowledged India's humanitarian and technical assistance following the devastating Rasuwa-Bhotekoshi floods, which caused extensive damage and loss of lives in Nepal.

Prime Minister Modi reiterated India's commitment to supporting Nepal's development, describing the country as a close friend and important neighbour.

The leaders also reviewed ongoing development initiatives, including High Impact Community Development Projects and the construction of Nepal's National Police Academy. They welcomed India's announcement of grant assistance for setting up an Agro-Industrial Park in Chitwan.

Major boost to India-Nepal transport connectivity

Improving cross-border connectivity and expanding trade infrastructure featured prominently in the bilateral discussions.

Prime Minister Modi announced a new transit trade route for Nepal through Deendayal Port at Kandla in Gujarat. Nepal will also gain access to Paradip Port in Odisha once the necessary infrastructure is developed.

In aviation, both countries agreed to work towards making the existing Kathmandu-Bhairahawa-Lucknow B345 air route operational in both directions. Technical consultations will determine the arrangements for implementing the proposal.

The leaders also flagged off the inaugural Sarayu-Kamala Express rail service connecting Ayodhya and Janakpur. The service is expected to strengthen religious tourism and improve travel along the Ramayana Circuit, which holds cultural and spiritual importance for people in both countries.

Another important development was the completion of the Field Location Survey for the proposed electrified broad-gauge railway line between Raxaul and Kathmandu. India and Nepal also agreed to cooperate in developing Nepal's East-West Railway network.

On road and border infrastructure, the two leaders virtually laid the foundation stone for an Integrated Check Post at Banbasa. They also inaugurated a new motorable cross-border bridge linking Dharchula in India with Darchula in Nepal.

These initiatives are expected to facilitate smoother movement of people and goods while strengthening economic links between the two countries.

Energy cooperation gains momentum with new projects

Energy emerged as another major pillar of the strengthened partnership, with several petroleum and hydropower initiatives receiving attention.

The two Prime Ministers dedicated Phase II of the Motihari-Amlekhgunj petroleum pipeline to the people of Nepal. They also virtually laid the foundation stone for a new petroleum terminal in Jhapa and called for the early implementation of the proposed Siliguri-Jhapa pipeline.

In the hydropower sector, the leaders virtually laid the foundation stone for the 669 MW Lower Arun Hydroelectric Project.

They also called for accelerating work on the Pancheshwar Multipurpose Project, while emphasising the need to speed up the preparation of Detailed Project Reports for the Saptakoshi and Sunkoshi multipurpose projects.

These projects are expected to support regional energy development, improve water resource management and contribute to flood mitigation efforts.

India to establish an IIT campus in Nepal

Educational cooperation received a major boost with India's agreement to establish an offshore campus of the Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) in Nepal.

The proposed institution is expected to strengthen Nepal's higher education infrastructure, expand access to advanced technical education and create opportunities for research, innovation and skilled employment.

India and Nepal also agreed to deepen their engagement in emerging technology, start-up development, digital public services and financial connectivity.

The broader objective is to create more opportunities for businesses, students, researchers and entrepreneurs in both countries.

Eight MoUs signed to expand bilateral cooperation

As part of the State Visit, India and Nepal exchanged eight Memoranda of Understanding (MoUs) covering education, research, security, healthcare, technology and institutional development.

The agreements cover the following areas:

  • Academic and research cooperation between the National Forensic Sciences University and Nepal Police.
  • Institutional cooperation between Rashtriya Raksha University and Nepal's National Police Academy.
  • Capacity-building and training programmes for Nepalese civil servants.
  • Cooperation in Ayurveda and traditional systems of medicine.
  • Establishment of a satellite communication network using the South Asia Satellite.
  • Technical cooperation in sustainable urban development.
  • Collaboration in healthcare and medical services.
  • Cooperation in livestock and fisheries development.

The two governments also agreed to improve access to banking, telecommunications and other essential services for their citizens living across the border.

The initiative is intended to make everyday services more accessible and further strengthen people-to-people connections.

Modi accepts invitation to visit Nepal

At the conclusion of the visit, Prime Minister Balendra Shah thanked the Indian government for its hospitality and extended an invitation to Prime Minister Narendra Modi to visit Nepal.

Prime Minister Modi accepted the invitation. The dates for the proposed visit will be finalised through diplomatic channels.

The latest agreements reflect the shared commitment of India and Nepal to deepen their longstanding relationship through infrastructure development, economic cooperation, energy partnerships and stronger regional connectivity.

With ₹15,000 crore in development assistance and a wide range of new initiatives, the two countries have outlined an expanded framework for cooperation aimed at supporting Nepal's development while strengthening the India-Nepal partnership.

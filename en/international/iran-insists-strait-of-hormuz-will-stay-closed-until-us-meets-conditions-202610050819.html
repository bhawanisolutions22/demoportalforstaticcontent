Iran insists Strait of Hormuz will stay closed until US meets conditions

Iran’s parliament speaker Mohammad Baqer Qalibaf declared on Sunday that the Strait of Hormuz will remain shut until Washington fulfills seven conditions outlined in a June memorandum of understanding.

Speaking after the UN General Assembly, Qalibaf stressed that Tehran’s stance is “clear and firm,” rejecting what he described as one‑sided demands from the United States. He said the reopening of the vital waterway depends on concrete steps from Washington, including security guarantees and sanctions relief.

Iran’s Foreign Ministry reiterated that nuclear issues are not part of the current talks, focusing instead on restoring safe passage through the strait. Spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei denied reports of nuclear concessions, saying Tehran’s priority is maritime security.

Qatar has been mediating between the two sides, with diplomats warning that the dispute could escalate if negotiations fail. Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi cautioned that Iran is prepared to respond if the US resumes military action, while the army signaled plans to extend missile ranges.

The Strait of Hormuz, a conduit for nearly 20% of global oil and gas shipments before the war, remains at the center of regional tensions.

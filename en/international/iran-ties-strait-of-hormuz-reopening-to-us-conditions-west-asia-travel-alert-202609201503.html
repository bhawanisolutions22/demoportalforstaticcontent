Iran Ties Strait of Hormuz Reopening to US Conditions; Washington Issues Fresh West Asia Travel Alert

Iran has declared that the Strait of Hormuz will remain closed until its conditions are met and the United States fulfills its commitments. Iranian Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, quoted by the semi‑official Fars News Agency, said Tehran would continue to “fight and negotiate,” combining military action with diplomacy to secure what it views as battlefield gains.

Iran’s conditions reportedly include an end to hostilities and changes to US sanctions and restrictions on Iranian shipping. The Strait of Hormuz, a critical global oil route, remains at the centre of the conflict.

The US CENTCOM reported assisting over 2,000 commercial vessels and facilitating the movement of more than one billion barrels of crude oil from the Gulf in the past two months. Despite these efforts, shipping activity remains significantly below pre‑war levels as Iran challenges US presence in the waterway.

Meanwhile, regional tensions continue to escalate. Saudi Arabia intercepted a ballistic missile aimed at Riyadh, while Yemen’s Houthi movement claimed responsibility for attacks on Saudi targets. In response, the US State Department renewed its travel alert for Americans in or traveling to West Asia, warning that the conflict involving Iran “has the potential to escalate rapidly.”

The advisory urges Americans to reconsider travel through the region amid heightened security risks and ongoing hostilities.

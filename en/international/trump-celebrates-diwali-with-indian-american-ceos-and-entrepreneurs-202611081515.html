Trump Celebrates Diwali at White House with Indian-American CEOs and Entrepreneurs

US President Donald Trump marked Diwali at the White House by hosting a gathering of prominent Indian-American business leaders, entrepreneurs, diplomats and senior members of his administration. The celebration brought together several influential personalities, including Micron Technology CEO Sanjay Mehrotra.

The event took place in the Oval Office on Friday, where Trump lit a traditional diya as part of the festivities. Among the distinguished guests were India's Ambassador to the United States, Vinay Mohan Kwatra; celebrity chef Vikas Khanna; entrepreneur Vishal Gondal; IBM CEO Arvind Krishna; FBI Director Kash Patel; and National Institutes of Health Director Jay Bhattacharya.

The gathering also included US Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Arjun Mody, Deputy Commissioner of the Social Security Administration.

Several Indian-American business leaders and entrepreneurs attended the occasion. They included Ruby Dhalla, President and CEO of Dhalla Group of Companies; Rajiv Sharma, founder and CEO of Knack Systems; Viswajith Mallampati, President of Keshavam DC; and Hetalkumar Patel, who attended as a guest of the President. White House Events Usher Arvind Chadha was also present.

Vishal Gondal Gifts Hanuman Chalisa to Trump

Entrepreneur Vishal Gondal, founder and CEO of GOQii Technologies, shared photographs from the celebration on social media, describing it as a memorable moment of shared light inside the Oval Office.

Gondal also presented President Trump with a copy of the Hanuman Chalisa, the devotional hymn dedicated to Lord Hanuman and traditionally recited by millions for courage, devotion and strength.

In his social media post, Gondal expressed his honour at celebrating Diwali with the US President. He also said that his prayer during the lighting of the diyas was for stronger friendship between India and the United States and for the victory of light over darkness.

The celebration highlighted the cultural contributions of the Indian-American community and its growing presence in business, technology, public service and other important fields in the United States.

History of Diwali Celebrations at the White House

The tradition of celebrating Diwali at the White House dates back to 2003, when President George W. Bush initiated the practice.

In the early years, celebrations were generally organised at the Eisenhower Executive Office Building, with Indian-American community representatives and Indian diplomats among the attendees.

A significant moment in the tradition came in 2009, when President Barack Obama became the first US President to personally light a diya at the White House. The ceremony took place in the East Room.

Since then, Diwali celebrations have been organised at different locations within the White House complex, including the Roosevelt Room and the Oval Office.

The latest celebration under President Trump continues this tradition, recognising Diwali as an important cultural occasion and reflecting the contributions of Indian-Americans to American society.

It also provided an opportunity to celebrate the longstanding cultural connections between India and the United States, with wishes for continued friendship, cooperation and prosperity between the two nations.

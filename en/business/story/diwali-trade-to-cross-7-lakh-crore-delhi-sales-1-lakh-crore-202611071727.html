Diwali Trade Expected to Cross ₹7 Lakh Crore Across India, Delhi Sales Top ₹1 Lakh Crore: CAIT

Festive shopping across India has given a major boost to the country's retail economy, with Diwali-related business expected to exceed ₹7 lakh crore this year. In Delhi alone, festive sales have crossed ₹1 lakh crore, according to the Confederation of All India Traders (CAIT).

In a statement issued on Saturday, CAIT reported that Diwali-related trade across the country had already surpassed ₹6 lakh crore, supported by strong consumer demand and heavy footfall in markets. Shopping activity reached its peak around Dhanteras, with several retail sectors recording significant sales.

Gold, electronics and clothing lead festive demand

Gold, silver and jewellery emerged as major contributors to festive shopping, alongside strong demand for electronic products and household appliances. Mobile phones, LED televisions, refrigerators, washing machines and air conditioners were among the popular purchases.

The clothing and fashion segment also experienced brisk business. Sarees, suits, kurtas, ready-made garments, children's clothing and traditional outfits remained in high demand as consumers prepared for Diwali celebrations.

Sales of traditional festive items also remained strong. Earthen diyas, idols of Goddess Lakshmi and Lord Ganesha, Pooja materials, sweets and other food products witnessed increased purchases.

The automobile industry also benefited from the festive mood, with cars, two-wheelers and commercial vehicles attracting customers. Handicrafts and locally manufactured products, including khadi, handloom goods and decorative items made by artisans and women's self-help groups, also recorded healthy demand.

Delhi crosses ₹1 lakh crore in Diwali business

CAIT Secretary General and Member of Parliament Praveen Khandelwal said Delhi's Diwali-related trade had crossed ₹1 lakh crore, while nationwide business had already exceeded ₹6 lakh crore.

He expressed confidence that the overall figure would cross ₹7 lakh crore as customer turnout and buying activity continued to remain strong.

According to CAIT, the surge in festive spending has benefited a wide range of businesses and workers. Small and medium-sized traders, street vendors, daily-wage workers, cottage industries, women entrepreneurs, artisans and self-help groups have all gained from increased consumer spending.

Transport operators, packaging companies, logistics providers and delivery services have also experienced additional business during the festive period.

Growing preference for Indian-made products

Khandelwal said the growing demand for domestic products reflected increasing consumer interest in Indian-made goods. He attributed this trend partly to the impact of Prime Minister Narendra Modi's appeals promoting 'Vocal for Local', 'Aatmanirbhar Bharat' and Swadeshi products.

Indian manufacturers, local entrepreneurs, artisans and small-scale industries have received greater consumer support during the festive season, he added.

Festive shopping brings momentum to Delhi markets

Commenting on the business environment in the capital, Khandelwal said the strong festive turnout had helped commercial activities regain momentum following losses that CAIT attributed to the Opposition rally on November 1 and protests and demonstrations held across Delhi during the previous month.

He said increased festive purchases had provided financial relief to millions of traders, shopkeepers, street vendors, daily-wage earners and small entrepreneurs.

Khandelwal also emphasised that peaceful protests and demonstrations are a democratic right, but such activities should not disrupt public movement or affect the livelihoods and daily earnings of traders and ordinary families.

On behalf of Delhi's trading community, CAIT thanked residents for their enthusiastic participation in this year's Diwali celebrations.

With nationwide business expected to cross ₹7 lakh crore, this year's festive shopping season has provided a significant boost to India's retail trade, domestic manufacturers and small businesses.

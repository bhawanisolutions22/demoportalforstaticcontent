PM Modi Pays Tribute to Guru Nanak Dev Ji on Parkash Purab

Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Monday paid tribute to Sri Guru Nanak Dev Ji on the occasion of his Parkash Purab, remembering the Sikh founder's timeless teachings of equality, devotion, selfless service and the welfare of humanity.

Speaking at a Shabad Kirtan programme in New Delhi, the Prime Minister extended his greetings to people across the country. He said that Gurbani and the melodies of kirtan represent much more than music, serving as a source of spiritual wisdom, knowledge and devotion.

Recalling Guru Nanak Dev Ji's guiding principles of 'Kirat Karo, Vand Chhako, Naam Japo' — earn through honest work, share with others and remember God — Modi said these teachings have remained an important part of his own life. He also spoke about his experiences visiting gurdwaras, participating in kirtan and taking part in seva and langar.

Remembering the Sacrifices of Sikh Gurus

This year's Parkash Purab celebrations hold additional significance as they coincide with the 350th anniversary of the martyrdom of Sri Guru Tegh Bahadur Ji. Remembering his supreme sacrifice in Delhi to protect freedom of faith and belief, the Prime Minister said his courage and legacy continue to inspire generations.

Modi also paid tribute to Bhai Mati Das Ji, Bhai Sati Das Ji and Bhai Dayala Ji, who sacrificed their lives while defending India's spiritual and cultural traditions.

He remembered Sardar Teja Singh Samundri for his contribution to the Gurdwara Reform Movement, noting that the country is observing the centenary of his martyrdom this year.

The Prime Minister highlighted the important role played by Sikh Gurus in preserving India's cultural identity during challenging periods in history. He said their teachings and sacrifices would continue to guide the nation's progress towards the goal of Viksit Bharat.

Government Initiatives to Preserve Sikh Heritage

Modi highlighted several initiatives undertaken over the past 12 years to commemorate the contributions of Sikh Gurus and make their legacy more accessible to younger generations.

These included celebrations marking the 350th Parkash Purab of Guru Gobind Singh Ji and the 550th Parkash Purab of Guru Nanak Dev Ji. He also recalled the national programme organised at the Red Fort to mark the 400th Parkash Purab of Guru Tegh Bahadur Ji and the ongoing observances commemorating the 350th anniversary of his martyrdom.

Referring to Veer Bal Diwas, observed on December 26, the Prime Minister said the occasion aims to introduce younger generations to the extraordinary courage and sacrifice of the Sahibzadas and Mata Gujri Ji.

He said their stories of bravery, faith and determination could inspire young Indians to work towards the country's progress and dedicate themselves to national service.

Modi also spoke about efforts to promote Sikh heritage internationally. Recalling his recent visit to the historic Khalsa Diwan Gurdwara in the Philippines, he said the Indian government was extending support for its redevelopment.

Call for Peace and Collective Fight Against Drug Abuse

Highlighting the Sikh prayer, 'Nanak Naam Chardi Kala, Tere Bhane Sarbat Da Bhala', Modi emphasised that the message of universal welfare remains particularly important at a time when several parts of the world are witnessing conflict and violence.

He called for a world where every individual and family can live with peace, security and dignity.

The Prime Minister also expressed concern over the growing challenge of drug abuse among young people. Describing substance abuse as a serious social concern, he urged families, gurdwaras, religious institutions and social organisations to work together to protect the younger generation.

He stressed the importance of guiding young people towards positive opportunities, healthy choices and meaningful aspirations.

Modi expressed confidence that collective action, inspired by the teachings of the Sikh Gurus, would help address social challenges and create a brighter future for India's youth.

He said the values of service, equality, compassion and universal brotherhood taught by the Sikh Gurus remain relevant today and can continue to contribute to a more peaceful and inclusive society.

India Expands Social Security Coverage to Protect Over One Billion People

India has significantly expanded its social security network, with more than one billion people now covered under various social protection programmes. According to the Ministry of Labour and Employment, the country's social protection coverage increased from 19% in 2015 to 68.4% in 2026.

The expansion is being supported by changes to provident fund eligibility, pension schemes, digital services and welfare programmes targeting workers in both organised and unorganised sectors.

One of the major developments is the increase in the mandatory Employees' Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO) wage ceiling from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 per month. The revision is expected to bring more than 51 lakh additional employees under EPFO coverage, subject to applicable provisions.

The government is also introducing measures to improve access to retirement savings, insurance, pension benefits and financial assistance for informal, gig and platform workers.

Here is a closer look at the major developments in India's social security framework.

What Is Social Security and Why Is It Important?

Social security refers to financial assistance and welfare protection designed to support people during periods of economic uncertainty and different stages of life.

It helps individuals and families manage financial challenges arising from retirement, illness, disability, loss of income and other vulnerable circumstances.

Traditionally, many social security benefits were linked to formal employment. However, with a growing number of people working in informal jobs, self-employment, gig services and platform-based occupations, extending protection to a wider workforce has become increasingly important.

India's evolving social security framework aims to make these benefits accessible to a much larger section of the population.

EPFO: Three Major Pillars of Employee Protection

The Employees' Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO), operating under the Ministry of Labour and Employment, plays a central role in providing retirement and insurance benefits to eligible employees.

Its social security framework is built around three major schemes:

1. Employees' Provident Fund (EPF)

The EPF scheme helps employees build long-term retirement savings through regular contributions from employees and employers, along with accumulated interest.

Members can also withdraw a portion of their provident fund savings for specified purposes, including medical treatment, education, marriage and housing, subject to applicable conditions.

2. Employees' Pension Scheme (EPS)

The EPS provides monthly pension benefits to eligible members after retirement or on account of disability.

In the event of a member's death, eligible family members, including spouses and children, may also receive pension benefits under the scheme.

3. Employees' Deposit Linked Insurance (EDLI)

The EDLI scheme provides insurance protection to eligible EPF members in the event of death while in service.

It offers insurance benefits of up to ₹7 lakh without requiring employees to pay a separate insurance premium.

How Has EPFO Expanded Its Coverage?

EPFO's membership, establishment network and pension coverage have grown considerably over the years.

According to government figures, the number of contributing EPFO members increased from 3.49 crore in 2014-15 to approximately 7.98 crore as of September 16, 2026.

The number of establishments contributing to EPFO reached 7.68 lakh in September 2026.

Pension coverage has also increased significantly. EPFO provided pension benefits to 51.04 lakh pensioners in 2014-15, compared with approximately 82 lakh pensioners as of September 2026.

The organisation has also expanded its claim settlement operations. The number of claims settled increased from 1.30 crore in 2014-15 to 6.42 crore during the one-year period up to October 2026.

These figures reflect the growing reach of India's formal retirement and employee protection system.

EPFO Wage Ceiling Increased to ₹25,000: What Employees Should Know

A significant step towards expanding provident fund coverage is the revision of the wage ceiling for mandatory EPFO membership.

The previous ceiling of ₹6,500 per month was increased to ₹15,000 in September 2014. In September 2026, the government revised it further to ₹25,000 per month.

The latest revision is expected to bring more than 51 lakh additional employees under mandatory EPFO coverage, subject to statutory eligibility requirements.

The change is intended to extend provident fund and associated social security benefits to more employees as wage levels and employment patterns evolve.

Easier PF Withdrawals and Faster Claim Settlements

EPFO has introduced several changes to simplify provident fund withdrawals and reduce administrative delays for members.

One major reform involves consolidating 13 existing provisions for partial withdrawals into three broader categories:

  • Essential Needs: Withdrawals for illness, education and marriage.
  • Housing Needs: Withdrawals for eligible housing-related requirements.
  • Special Circumstances: Withdrawals permitted under other specified conditions.

The revised framework has also increased the permitted withdrawal limits to 10 times for education and five times for marriage. Previously, members had a combined limit of three withdrawals for these two purposes.

Under the framework introduced in October 2025, eligible members can withdraw up to 75% of their PF balance, including employee and employer contributions, subject to prescribed conditions.

The minimum service requirement has also been standardised at 12 months.

Other important changes include:

  • The auto-settlement limit for eligible advance claims has been increased to ₹5 lakh.
  • Eligible online claim applicants no longer need to upload cheque-leaf images or attested bank passbooks.
  • Simplified procedures aim to reduce paperwork and improve access to provident fund savings.

These measures are intended to make EPFO services more convenient and accessible.

VISHWAS and AMNESTY Schemes Aim to Simplify EPFO Compliance

Apart from reforms targeting employees, EPFO has introduced initiatives to help employers address compliance issues and reduce administrative difficulties.

VISHWAS, 2026

The VISHWAS scheme provides a one-time settlement mechanism for eligible disputes involving damages or penalties under the Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952, and the Code on Social Security, 2020.

The initiative aims to reduce litigation and encourage voluntary compliance through a digital and time-bound process.

Eligible defaults occurring before June 14, 2024, may qualify for reduced monthly damage rates, depending on the period of delay and applicable conditions.

AMNESTY, 2026

The AMNESTY scheme is designed to help eligible establishments regularise provident fund trusts that have recognition under the Income Tax Act but do not have formal exemption under the relevant EPF legislation.

It provides an opportunity to obtain retrospective exemption, subject to prescribed requirements.

The scheme is scheduled to remain open until December 28, 2026.

How Digital Reforms Are Transforming EPFO Services

Digitalisation is becoming an important part of India's social security delivery system. EPFO has introduced technology-based services to simplify registration, pension payments, withdrawals and claim processing.

Since November 2019, employees have been able to generate their Universal Account Number (UAN) independently.

In April 2025, EPFO introduced UAN generation and activation through Aadhaar-based Face Authentication Technology using the UMANG application.

By October 2026, approximately 1.74 crore UANs had been self-generated.

The UAN is a permanent identification number that connects an employee's provident fund accounts across different establishments. It helps maintain continuity of PF records when employees change jobs, subject to verification.

Other major digital initiatives include:

Centralised Pension Payment System (CPPS)

CPPS allows pensioners to receive their pension through any bank branch across the country. Pensioners do not need to transfer their Pension Payment Order when changing their location or bank branch.

Doorstep Digital Life Certificate

EPFO, in partnership with India Post Payments Bank, provides doorstep Digital Life Certificate services to pensioners.

The service costs ₹50 per certificate, with the expense borne by EPFO.

Redesigned EPFO Website

Launched on November 1, 2025, the redesigned mobile-responsive website provides access to provident fund passbooks, claim forms, pension services and profile updates.

EPFO 3.0

EPFO 3.0 aims to modernise the organisation's operations through core banking-based technology, automated claim processing, multilingual self-service facilities and faster service delivery.

Under the initiative, risk-validated claims are designed to be processed in less than three days, compared with earlier processing periods of up to 20 days.

e-Shram and Other Schemes Extend Protection to Unorganised Workers

India's social security expansion is not limited to employees working in the organised sector. Several initiatives are designed to reach informal workers, self-employed individuals and people without conventional employer-employee arrangements.

e-Shram Portal

Launched in August 2021, the e-Shram portal created India's first Aadhaar-seeded National Database of Unorganised Workers.

The platform had recorded 31.89 crore registrations as of August 2026.

The e-Shram One-Stop Solution, launched in October 2024, brings together 15 social security and welfare schemes.

It also connects workers with other government platforms, including the National Career Service, Skill India Digital Hub and myScheme.

Pradhan Mantri Shram Yogi Maandhan (PM-SYM)

PM-SYM is a voluntary contributory pension scheme designed for eligible unorganised workers.

It targets individuals aged between 18 and 40 years with monthly incomes of up to ₹15,000.

Eligible subscribers receive an assured minimum monthly pension of ₹3,000 after reaching the age of 60.

Monthly contributions range from ₹55 to ₹200, depending on the subscriber's age at enrolment. The Central Government makes an equal contribution.

The scheme aims to provide a source of regular income for eligible workers during retirement.

Pradhan Mantri Samajik Sahayata Yojana: Financial Support for Vulnerable Groups

The Pradhan Mantri Samajik Sahayata Yojana, revised from the existing National Social Assistance Programme (NSAP) in October 2026, aims to provide financial assistance to eligible elderly people, widows, persons with disabilities and families facing the loss of their primary breadwinner.

The programme is scheduled to continue from 2026-27 to 2030-31.

Its major benefits include:

Old-Age Pension

  • ₹200 per month for eligible beneficiaries aged 60-79 years.
  • ₹500 per month for those aged 80 years and above.

Widow Pension

  • ₹300 per month for eligible widows aged 40-79 years.
  • ₹500 per month for those aged 80 years and above.

Disability Pension

  • ₹375 per month for eligible beneficiaries below 80 years.
  • ₹625 per month for beneficiaries aged 80 years and above.

The revised framework also reduces the benchmark disability threshold from 80% to 40% and removes the minimum age requirement.

Family Benefit

Eligible households receive one-time financial assistance of ₹20,000 following the death of their primary breadwinner, provided the deceased was aged between 18 and 59 years.

These benefits are intended to provide financial assistance to vulnerable households.

Code on Social Security, 2020: A Unified Framework for Workers

The Code on Social Security, 2020, consolidates nine existing central labour laws relating to social security into a single legislative framework.

It covers several important areas of worker protection, including provident fund, pensions, insurance, maternity benefits and gratuity.

An important feature of the Code is its recognition of the changing nature of employment. It includes provisions for extending social security benefits to unorganised workers, gig workers and platform workers.

The Code also provides for social security schemes for gig and platform workers, supported through a dedicated social security fund.

Other provisions include:

  • Gratuity for fixed-term employees: Eligible fixed-term employees can receive gratuity on a proportionate basis after completing one year of service, subject to applicable conditions.
  • Maternity benefits: Eligible women employees can receive maternity benefits of up to 26 weeks.
  • Work-from-home provisions: The framework provides for work-from-home arrangements after maternity leave where the nature of the work permits and applicable conditions are met.
  • Crèche facilities: Establishments meeting prescribed conditions are required to provide crèche facilities.

These provisions are intended to broaden the reach of social security and address the needs of workers across different employment categories.

What India's Expanding Social Security Network Means for Workers

India's social security framework is undergoing significant changes, with greater emphasis on wider coverage, simplified services and technology-driven delivery.

The expansion from 19% coverage in 2015 to 68.4% in 2026 represents a substantial increase in the number of people included within the country's social protection system.

Higher EPFO wage eligibility, improved PF withdrawal facilities, pension reforms, e-Shram registrations and welfare assistance programmes are among the measures contributing to this expansion.

The broader policy objective is to ensure that financial protection is not limited to conventional salaried employees but gradually reaches informal workers, gig professionals, platform workers, elderly citizens and other vulnerable groups.

As these initiatives develop, their effective implementation and accessibility will be important in determining how widely workers and their families benefit from India's expanding social security network.

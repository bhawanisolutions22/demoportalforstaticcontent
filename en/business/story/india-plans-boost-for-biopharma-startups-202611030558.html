India Plans Boost for Biopharma Startups

The Indian government is exploring a series of measures to strengthen the country's biopharmaceutical and biotechnology startup ecosystem, with a focus on removing regulatory hurdles, improving access to funding and accelerating innovation.

Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal held discussions with representatives of India's biopharma and biotechnology startups on Monday, focusing on the challenges facing emerging companies and opportunities to expand the sector.

During the interaction, Goyal indicated that the government is considering coordinated efforts to simplify regulatory procedures, speed up patent approvals, improve funding opportunities and strengthen domestic procurement systems and research infrastructure.

He emphasised that closer cooperation between the government, private industry and research institutions would help innovative startups expand their operations and contribute more significantly to India's growing bioeconomy.

India's Bioeconomy Crosses $195 Billion

India's biotechnology sector has witnessed substantial growth over the past decade. According to the India BioEconomy Report (IBER) 2026, the country's bioeconomy has expanded from approximately $10 billion in 2014 to more than $195 billion in 2025.

The sector recorded annual growth of around 17–18 per cent over the past year, increasing from approximately $165 billion to $195 billion. This growth has strengthened India's position as an emerging global biotechnology and biomanufacturing hub.

The bioeconomy now contributes approximately 4.8 per cent to India's Gross Domestic Product (GDP), with a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of nearly 18 per cent.

The industry has more than doubled in size since 2020 and is supported by over 11,800 biotechnology startups operating across the country.

Government Targets $300 Billion Bioeconomy by 2030

India is working towards an ambitious target of building a $300 billion bioeconomy by 2030. A growing pool of scientific talent, entrepreneurs, research institutions and technology-driven startups is expected to play an important role in achieving this goal.

However, further expansion will require stronger research capabilities, easier access to capital, faster commercialisation of new technologies and a supportive regulatory environment.

BioE3 Policy to Strengthen Biotechnology Innovation

The Centre's BioE3 Policy, which stands for Biotechnology for Economy, Environment and Employment, is a key initiative aimed at accelerating innovation and promoting sustainable biomanufacturing in India.

The policy focuses on emerging areas such as:

  • Precision biotherapeutics and advanced healthcare solutions.
  • Smart proteins and alternative food technologies.
  • Climate-resilient agriculture and sustainable farming.
  • Bio-based chemicals and environmentally friendly materials.
  • Carbon capture and other climate-focused technologies.

Goyal also highlighted the role of the Biotechnology Industry Research Assistance Council (BIRAC) in supporting the sector's development.

BIRAC is helping establish shared research and manufacturing infrastructure, including biofoundries, Bio-AI hubs and advanced biomanufacturing platforms. These facilities are expected to help startups access specialised technology and move innovative ideas towards commercial production.

₹1 Lakh Crore RDI Fund to Support Deep-Tech Startups

The government has also established a Research, Development and Innovation (RDI) Fund with an allocation of ₹1 lakh crore to strengthen India's research and innovation ecosystem.

BIRAC has been assigned an important role in supporting biotechnology ventures under this initiative. The fund is intended to encourage long-term investment in research-driven businesses, particularly projects that are ready for technological development, commercialisation and large-scale expansion.

By directing capital towards promising technologies and innovation-led businesses, the initiative aims to strengthen India's deep-tech ecosystem and improve the ability of domestic companies to compete globally.

What the New Measures Could Mean for Biopharma Startups

The proposed support measures could address several longstanding challenges faced by biotechnology entrepreneurs, particularly startups working on advanced medicines, biological products and research-intensive technologies.

  • Faster approvals: Simplified regulatory procedures and quicker patent processing could reduce delays for innovative businesses.
  • Better funding access: Government-backed investment initiatives could help startups finance research and scale-up activities.
  • Improved infrastructure: Shared biofoundries and advanced manufacturing facilities could reduce the cost of accessing specialised equipment.
  • Stronger domestic capabilities: Greater support for indigenous procurement and research could encourage local innovation.
  • Global competitiveness: Better collaboration between industry, government and research institutions could help Indian biotechnology companies expand internationally.

With its bioeconomy already exceeding $195 billion and a target of $300 billion by 2030, India is positioning biotechnology as an important driver of economic growth, scientific advancement and employment generation.

The government's proposed measures, combined with the BioE3 Policy and the ₹1 lakh crore RDI Fund, could provide additional support to startups looking to turn scientific research into commercially viable products.

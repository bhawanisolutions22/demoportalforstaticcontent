Nitin Gadkari Calls for Safer and Greener Roads

Posted Date 20 Nov 2026

Union Minister for Road Transport and Highways Nitin Gadkari has called for stronger road safety measures, improved construction standards and wider adoption of sustainable technologies to strengthen India's road infrastructure.

Addressing the 85th Annual Session of the Indian Roads Congress (IRC) in Hyderabad, Telangana, on Thursday, Gadkari urged engineers, contractors, consultants and policymakers to work collectively towards building safer, stronger and environmentally responsible roads across the country.

The event was attended by Telangana Chief Minister A. Revanth Reddy, Roads and Buildings Minister Komatireddy Venkat Reddy, IRC President L. K. Padhi, senior officials from the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways and representatives from the engineering and infrastructure sectors.

Gadkari appreciated the contribution of engineers, contractors and government officials in expanding India's National Highway network. He said continued research, technical innovation, professional development and the adoption of international best practices would be essential for meeting the country's future infrastructure requirements.

Road Construction Must Prioritise Quality and Accountability

Highlighting the important role played by the Indian Roads Congress in developing technical standards for the road sector, Gadkari encouraged the organisation to strengthen international cooperation and facilitate greater exchange of knowledge and technology.

He emphasised that road quality, durability and public safety must remain central to every infrastructure project. Contractors, consultants and engineers should be held accountable for their responsibilities, and any compromise in construction standards should invite appropriate action.

The Minister also stressed the need for better Detailed Project Reports (DPRs), effective technical supervision and timely completion of highway projects. He called for stronger road safety audits and scientific planning to identify and address accident-prone locations.

According to Gadkari, improved junction designs, flyovers and underpasses, wherever necessary, can help reduce road accidents. He urged road engineers to focus on eliminating accident black spots through better engineering solutions and infrastructure planning.

Alternative Materials and Green Technologies for Highways

Gadkari called for innovative construction methods that can lower project costs without affecting the quality and durability of roads.

He encouraged the use of alternative construction materials, including plastic waste, used tyres, steel slag, municipal solid waste and agricultural residue, wherever technically suitable.

The Minister also highlighted the potential of bio-bitumen produced from rice straw. He said research and practical applications of such materials could support more sustainable highway development while reducing dependence on conventional resources.

He further encouraged the adoption of cleaner energy sources in construction equipment. These include electricity, ethanol, biodiesel, compressed natural gas (CNG), liquefied natural gas (LNG) and hydrogen-based technologies.

Wider use of these alternatives could help reduce pollution and dependence on imported fossil fuels. Gadkari also pointed to the potential economic benefits for farmers through stronger connections between agriculture and the energy sector.

On environmental conservation, he called for greater use of treated wastewater in construction activities, particularly in areas facing water shortages. He also encouraged recycling municipal waste, reusing available resources and conducting further research into tree transplantation and plantation along highways.

Better Road Connectivity to Support Economic Growth

Gadkari highlighted the importance of road infrastructure in strengthening India's economy. Better roads and improved connectivity can reduce travel time and logistics expenses while supporting agriculture, manufacturing, trade, tourism and other economic activities.

Referring to the Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana (PMGSY), he emphasised the need to provide rural communities with reliable, all-weather road connectivity. Better rural roads can improve access to markets, healthcare, education and employment opportunities.

The Minister also called for exploring additional financing options to accelerate infrastructure development. Public-private partnerships and the monetisation of existing highway assets were among the mechanisms he highlighted for mobilising resources.

Indian Roads Congress Asked to Strengthen Standards

Gadkari urged the Indian Roads Congress to focus not only on preparing technical codes, manuals and guidelines but also on ensuring their effective implementation throughout the country.

He called for stronger coordination between Central and State Governments, research institutions, engineering professionals and industry stakeholders to address emerging challenges in the road infrastructure sector.

Expressing confidence in the discussions at the 85th Annual Session, Gadkari said the recommendations and technical exchanges would contribute to India's efforts to develop safer, more durable, sustainable and technologically advanced roads.

The emphasis on road safety, construction accountability, alternative materials and cleaner technologies reflects the broader need to develop infrastructure that supports economic growth while protecting human lives and the environment.

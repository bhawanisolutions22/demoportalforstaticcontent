Kashmir’s Last Local Terrorist Killed: What It Means for Security in the Valley

Posted Date 22 Nov 2026

The killing of Mohammad Lateef Bhat, a 22-year-old Lashkar-e-Taiba (LeT) terrorist, has marked a significant development in the security situation in Jammu and Kashmir. His death has reportedly left the Kashmir Valley without any active local terrorists for the first time since militancy began in 1989.

Bhat was killed during an encounter with security forces in Zanglipora, Kulgam district, on November 18. The operation took place a day after his associate, Umar Nisar Dar, was also killed.

Security officials believe Bhat was the last active local terrorist operating in the Valley. However, authorities are cautious about declaring an end to militancy, as previous periods of declining terrorist activity have been followed by renewed recruitment and violence.

A Significant Development in Kashmir’s Security Situation

The decline in local militancy is not entirely unprecedented. In 2013, the number of active terrorists in Kashmir reportedly fell below 80, with fewer than 35 being local recruits. South Kashmir, which later became a major centre of militancy, had also experienced a considerable reduction in terrorist activity during that period.

Despite these earlier improvements, militancy eventually returned, highlighting the challenges involved in maintaining long-term security.

According to a senior police officer involved in counter-insurgency operations, Bhat had recently been attempting to recruit new members. His associate, Umar Nisar Dar, was among those believed to have joined his network.

Officials believe Bhat's elimination could disrupt immediate recruitment efforts and prevent the formation of a new local terrorist group.

Foreign Terrorist Operatives Remain a Major Concern

Although local terrorist activity in the Valley has reportedly been eliminated, security agencies continue to monitor foreign operatives associated with Lashkar-e-Taiba.

Police sources estimate that around 30 LeT operatives remain active in Kashmir, with many believed to be operating from forested and mountainous areas.

Security officials point out that local networks have historically played an important role in helping foreign terrorists with shelter, intelligence, movement and logistical support.

Without such assistance, planning and carrying out large-scale attacks could become more difficult for foreign operatives.

However, the continued presence of these groups means that security forces cannot reduce their vigilance.

Concerns Over Circulation of Bhat’s Final Video

The circulation of a video showing Bhat's final conversation with senior police and Army officers has also raised concerns among security officials.

The recording reportedly showed security personnel giving him an opportunity to surrender before the encounter ended.

While the video may have been circulated to demonstrate that security forces provided an opportunity for surrender, some officers are concerned about its possible unintended consequences.

According to a police officer operating in South Kashmir, the recording could influence young people in different ways. It might discourage recruitment by demonstrating that those who join militancy can eventually be tracked down. However, authorities are also concerned that the footage could have an adverse impact on vulnerable individuals.

Preventing fresh recruitment and ensuring that another generation of local terrorists does not emerge remain key priorities for security agencies.

Jammu Continues to Have Active Local Terrorists

While the Kashmir Valley is believed to have no active local terrorists following Bhat's death, the situation in the Jammu region remains different.

At least three local terrorists are reportedly active in Jammu, all believed to be from Kishtwar district. They include Mohammad Amin, also known as Jahangir Saroori.

Security agencies therefore continue to face challenges on multiple fronts, particularly in areas south of the Pir Panjal mountain range.

What Lies Ahead for Security Forces?

Bhat's killing represents an important development in the ongoing counter-terrorism campaign in Kashmir. However, the absence of local terrorists does not necessarily mean that the threat of militancy has disappeared.

Foreign terrorist operatives, possible recruitment attempts, cross-regional networks and the risk of renewed local support remain important concerns.

For security forces, the immediate priority will be to prevent fresh recruitment, dismantle remaining terrorist networks and ensure that the decline in local militancy is sustained.

The coming months will be important in determining whether the current situation marks a lasting change in Kashmir's security landscape.

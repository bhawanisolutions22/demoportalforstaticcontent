India Reports 38% Drop in TB Deaths, Treatment Success Reaches 94%

Posted Date 13 Nov 2026

India has made significant progress in its fight against tuberculosis (TB), recording a 38 per cent reduction in TB-related deaths since 2015, according to the World Health Organization's (WHO) Global Tuberculosis Report 2026, released on November 12.

The decline is higher than the global reduction of 35 per cent over the same period. India has also achieved a treatment success rate of 94 per cent among patients with drug-susceptible TB, reflecting improvements in diagnosis, treatment delivery and patient support.

The progress has been driven by the National TB Elimination Programme (NTEP), which combines early detection, modern diagnostic technologies, timely treatment and community participation.

Government initiatives such as AI-enabled chest X-rays, rapid molecular diagnostic tests, nutritional assistance and digital patient monitoring have strengthened the country's TB response.

India Strengthens TB Detection Through Technology and Patient Support

India has expanded its TB detection efforts by introducing technology-based screening and improving access to diagnostic facilities, particularly in high-risk communities.

Artificial intelligence-enabled chest X-rays are helping identify suspected TB cases, while rapid diagnostic technologies are supporting faster confirmation and treatment initiation.

The government has also introduced AI-powered tools to identify disease hotspots, predict high-risk cases and improve communication with patients. Initiatives such as the Khushi AI chatbot and avatar videobot are part of these efforts.

Patient support remains another important part of the national strategy. Under the Ni-kshay Poshan Yojana, eligible TB patients receive financial assistance for nutritional requirements during treatment.

The Ni-kshay Mitra initiative encourages individuals, organisations and institutions to provide nutritional and psychosocial support to people affected by TB.

TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan Covers More Than 25 Crore People

The TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan has significantly expanded screening activities, focusing on vulnerable communities and people at higher risk of infection.

The first intensified 100-day campaign began on December 7, 2024, covering 347 high-priority districts across 33 States and Union Territories. Following its initial implementation, the campaign was expanded nationwide.

A second intensified 100-day drive was launched on March 24, 2026. It focused on 1.58 lakh high-risk villages and wards, along with congregate settings and vulnerable population groups.

Between December 7, 2024, and November 8, 2026, more than 25 crore people were brought under the campaign's screening activities.

During this period, the programme recorded:

  • More than 25 crore people covered through TB screening.
  • Over 11 crore chest X-rays conducted.
  • More than 3 crore nucleic acid amplification tests (NAATs) performed.
  • 48.64 lakh new TB cases identified.
  • 14.97 lakh asymptomatic TB cases detected.

The identification of asymptomatic cases is particularly important because people with TB may not always show noticeable symptoms. Proactive screening helps detect infections earlier, allowing treatment to begin before the disease progresses or spreads further.

The programme also emphasises timely treatment, nutritional assistance and continued patient monitoring. In 2025, a total of 67,933 panchayats were declared TB-free.

Advanced Diagnostics and Digital Platforms Improve TB Care

Technology is playing an increasingly important role in India's efforts to improve TB diagnosis and treatment.

Portable digital X-ray machines equipped with artificial intelligence are helping healthcare teams screen people in different geographical settings. Rapid molecular diagnostic technologies, including NAAT, are also improving the speed and accuracy of diagnosis.

The Ni-kshay digital platform supports patient registration, tracking and treatment monitoring. It helps healthcare authorities maintain continuity of care and identify patients who may require additional support.

India has also integrated TB screening with the identification and management of other health conditions, including diabetes and undernutrition. This approach helps healthcare providers address conditions that may complicate TB treatment.

Nutritional and psychosocial assistance remains central to the programme. Through Ni-kshay Poshan Yojana and Ni-kshay Mitra, the government and community partners are working to ensure that patients receive support throughout their treatment journey.

WHO Report Shows TB Mortality Falling to 18 per Lakh

The WHO report highlights a substantial decline in India's estimated TB mortality rate over the past decade.

According to the report, the estimated mortality rate decreased from 28 deaths per lakh population in 2015 to 18 deaths per lakh population in 2025, representing a reduction of 38 per cent.

The mortality estimates draw on several sources, including the Registrar General of India's Sample Registration System (RGI-SRS), community-based verbal autopsy assessments and programme information available through the Ni-kshay portal.

India's scientific research efforts are also contributing to improved TB surveillance.

The Indian Council of Medical Research–National Institute for Research in Tuberculosis (ICMR-NIRT) conducted a nationally representative TB prevalence survey in 2025. Its findings have been shared with WHO and are currently undergoing technical discussions.

Following these discussions, WHO is expected to release an updated country profile for India.

Community Participation Supports India's TB Elimination Mission

Community involvement has become an important part of India's TB elimination strategy, with local institutions, healthcare providers and citizens supporting awareness, screening and patient care.

Panchayati Raj Institutions, urban local bodies, elected representatives, civil society organisations, private healthcare providers and corporate partners are contributing to grassroots-level activities.

The Ni-kshay Mitra initiative has also encouraged citizens and organisations to support TB patients through nutritional and psychosocial assistance.

To strengthen youth participation, the government is promoting the JOSH Teams initiative. Teams are being formed at block and ward levels under the leadership of designated Class-I officers.

Each team is supported by five National Cadet Corps (NCC) cadets and five MY Bharat volunteers. Their responsibilities include spreading awareness, encouraging screening and mobilising communities to participate in TB elimination activities.

India's TB programme is also supported by strong domestic funding. According to the government, 96 per cent of the funding required for the programme comes from domestic resources.

India Reaffirms Commitment to a TB-Free Nation

The latest WHO findings highlight India's progress in reducing TB-related deaths, improving treatment outcomes and expanding access to screening and diagnosis.

The 38 per cent reduction in TB mortality, 94 per cent treatment success rate and large-scale screening efforts represent important developments in the country's fight against the disease.

The government plans to continue strengthening early detection, expanding access to quality diagnostics, improving treatment availability and increasing nutritional and preventive care.

With continued investment in healthcare infrastructure, technology, scientific research and community participation, India is working towards its goal of achieving a TB-Mukt Bharat.

The country will also continue collaborating with WHO, research institutions, States and Union Territories, development partners and communities to advance national and global TB elimination targets.

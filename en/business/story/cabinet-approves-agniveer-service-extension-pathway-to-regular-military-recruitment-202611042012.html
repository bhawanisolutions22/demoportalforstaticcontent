Cabinet approves two-year Agniveer service extension with route to permanent armed forces recruitment

The Union Cabinet has approved a significant revision to the Agnipath recruitment programme, allowing Agniveers who are not selected for regular service after completing their initial four-year tenure to continue serving for an additional two years.

Under the new arrangement, the existing system of selecting up to 25 per cent of Agniveers for direct regular service based on merit will remain unchanged. However, the remaining Agniveers will now have the option of undertaking a further two-year tenure, subject to their willingness to continue in service.

Government officials said that after successfully completing this additional period, eligible Agniveers will be given an opportunity to join the regular armed forces, provided they meet prescribed selection standards and choose to continue their military careers.

The Cabinet's decision follows a comprehensive review of the first four years of the Agnipath scheme. According to the government, feedback from the armed forces has been encouraging, with Agniveers demonstrating strong professional skills, discipline, physical fitness and adaptability to modern military technologies.

The additional engagement period, described as a "Second Tour of Duty", will offer higher pay, enhanced Seva Nidhi benefits and seniority-related advantages. Officials believe these incentives will improve career prospects for young personnel while strengthening the overall talent pool available to the armed forces.

Defence Minister Rajnath Singh welcomed the Cabinet's decision and thanked Prime Minister Narendra Modi for supporting the reform. In a post on social media platform X, Singh said the revised framework would allow motivated and qualified Agniveers to secure regular military positions either after four years or after completing the extended six-year engagement period.

He said the move is part of broader efforts to modernise India's defence forces by creating a younger, fitter and technologically capable military prepared for future security challenges.

According to Singh, the initiative reflects the government's long-term vision of adapting the armed forces to the evolving nature of warfare while providing committed Agniveers with a clearer pathway to long-term military service.

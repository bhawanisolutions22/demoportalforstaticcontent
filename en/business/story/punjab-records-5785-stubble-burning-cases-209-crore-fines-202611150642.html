Punjab Reports 5,785 Stubble Burning Cases; Fines Cross Rs 2 Crore

Posted Date 15 Nov 2026

Punjab has reported 5,785 stubble burning incidents during the ongoing paddy harvesting season, despite nearly 98 per cent of harvesting work being completed across the state. Authorities have imposed environmental compensation penalties worth Rs 2.09 crore as part of their efforts to control crop residue burning and reduce air pollution.

According to the latest report issued by the Punjab Pollution Control Board (PPCB) on November 13, officials inspected 5,312 agricultural fields to identify cases of stubble burning. Of these, 2,347 incidents were physically verified.

Following the inspections, authorities registered 2,095 environmental compensation cases and imposed penalties totalling Rs 2,09,15,000. However, only Rs 39,35,000 has been recovered so far.

Enforcement measures have also included the registration of 1,669 FIRs, red entries in 1,971 land revenue records and the issuance of 1,603 warning notices to individuals found violating the rules.

Patiala Reports 388 Stubble Burning Incidents

Patiala district has recorded 388 stubble burning incidents, even as approximately 98 per cent of its paddy harvesting has been completed.

District officials inspected 340 fields and physically confirmed fire incidents at 217 locations. A total of 162 environmental compensation cases were registered, resulting in fines of Rs 8.15 lakh. Of this amount, Rs 3 lakh has been recovered.

The district also registered 209 FIRs and made red entries in 136 land revenue records. Authorities issued 390 warning notices in connection with stubble burning violations.

Ferozepur Records Highest Number of Incidents

Among Punjab's districts, Ferozepur reported the highest number of stubble burning incidents, with 794 cases. Tarn Taran followed with 695 incidents, while Sangrur recorded 674.

Other districts also reported significant numbers of cases. Patiala recorded 388 incidents, Ludhiana reported 300 and Fazilka registered 211.

The PPCB report further noted that, as of November 13, no charge sheets had been issued and no suspension orders had been passed against nodal or supervisory officers in connection with stubble burning management across the state.

Monitoring Continues as Harvesting Nears Completion

With almost all paddy harvesting operations nearing completion, authorities continue to monitor agricultural fields and take action against those found burning crop residue.

The state government's enforcement measures include financial penalties, police cases, revenue record entries and warning notices. However, the recovery of only a portion of the imposed compensation highlights the continuing challenge of enforcing regulations against stubble burning.

The issue remains a major environmental concern in Punjab, particularly during the post-harvest period when crop residue fires can contribute to deteriorating air quality across northern India.
